This study reproduced the ramp-metering experiment of Zhou et al. using a
reimplemented CTM simulation environment, benchmark controllers, and RL training
pipeline. The no-control and reference PI-ALINEA scenarios were reproduced
closely, indicating that the reconstructed simulation environment captures the
main dynamics of the original study.

The reported superiority of the RL controller could not be confirmed once
stronger benchmark controllers were introduced. The selected RL policy achieved
stable density tracking and competitive mainline efficiency, but its performance
was comparable to PI-ALINEA optimised for \(\bar{v}\) and clearly inferior to
PI-ALINEA optimised for \(\bar{v}_{\mathrm{queue}}\) when ramp queueing delay was
included. The RL controller therefore appears effective for the objective it was
trained on, namely control-cell density tracking, but not necessarily superior
from a system-wide perspective.

The results emphasise three main lessons. First, benchmark controllers must be
carefully calibrated before claims of RL superiority can be assessed. Second,
reward design strongly determines the behaviour of learned controllers; omitting
queue delay encourages policies that may stabilise the mainline by transferring
delay to the ramp. Third, reproducibility requires more complete reporting than was provided in
the original article, particularly regarding demand profiles, discretisation
choices, learning schedules, and checkpoint-selection procedures. Most
importantly, quantitative performance indicators should be reported alongside
graphical results to enable direct comparison.

Future work should train the RL agent on randomised demand profiles, include
ramp queueing delay directly in the reward function, and compare against
optimised classical controllers under multiple demand scenarios and random
seeds. Such an evaluation would better determine whether value-function
approximation provides a genuine advantage over well-tuned feedback ramp-metering
controllers for distant downstream bottlenecks.
